# 1. Short State Summary Card

**Karnataka is generally more open today for Indian resident buyers of private agricultural land than it was before the 2020 land-reform changes, but every transaction must still be checked for land ceiling, RTC/Pahani, Mutation Register, 11E/survey sketch, Encumbrance Certificate, land conversion, access, PTCL/granted-land restrictions, and special-category land risks.**

Karnataka land purchases should not be evaluated only from a simple “farmer / non-farmer” eligibility angle. The larger risks are usually **whether the seller has clear title, whether the RTC/Pahani and mutation records match the sale deed and possession, whether the land is granted land / PTCL-sensitive land, whether a valid 11E sketch or survey record exists for a subdivided parcel, whether land conversion is required, whether the land is inside a planning area, and whether the land has access, encumbrance, family, inheritance, or litigation issues**.

The Karnataka Land Reforms (Amendment) Ordinance, 2020 omitted Sections 79A, 79B and 79C of the Karnataka Land Reforms Act, 1961, and changed the older framework that restricted certain non-agriculturists from holding agricultural land. However, the amendment did **not** remove all agricultural-land risks. Ceiling-area limits, restrictions on certain categories of land, PTCL/granted-land restrictions, conversion/planning approvals, and document/title checks still matter.

## Result: NRI / OCI Buyer

**Preliminary result: Restricted for agricultural land, plantation property, and farmhouse purchase. Legal review required.**

NRIs and persons of Indian origin are generally permitted to acquire residential and commercial immovable property in India, but the general permission does **not** cover agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property. The Ministry of External Affairs’ guidance states that an NRI can purchase immovable property in India other than agricultural land, plantation property, or farm house, and that acquisition of agricultural land / plantation property / farmhouse by NRI/PIO requires specific approval.

An NRI/PIO may inherit agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property, but transfer of such inherited property is restricted; the MEA guidance says agricultural land / plantation property / farmhouse acquired by inheritance may be transferred only to Indian citizens permanently residing in India.

**Source:** [MEA guidance on acquisition and transfer of immovable property in India](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf)

**Recommended next step:**  
Do not proceed with purchase without a FEMA + Karnataka land-law review.

# 3. Karnataka Legal Guidance: Main Content

## Buying Agricultural Land in Karnataka: What You Must Check

Buying agricultural land in Karnataka is different from buying an apartment, residential plot, or urban property. A buyer should not rely only on the listing, seller statement, broker statement, or RTC name.

In India, land ownership is generally presumptive: PRS notes that ownership is primarily established through registered sale deeds and supporting records, but these documents are not a government-guaranteed title, and the burden of checking past ownership usually falls on the buyer.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

For Karnataka, the practical legal check should start with eight questions:

1. **Is the seller legally entitled to sell?**
2. **Does the RTC/Pahani match the sale deed, mutation record, survey record, and ground possession?**
3. **Is the land private agricultural land or government / gomala / granted / PTCL-sensitive / forest / tank-bed / restricted land?**
4. **Is the buyer allowed to acquire this land type?**
5. **Does the buyer’s proposed use require land conversion or planning approval?**
6. **Is there a valid 11E sketch, phodi, durasth, or survey subdivision record where the parcel has been split?**
7. **Does the land have legal access from a public road or legally enforceable access path?**
8. **Are there encumbrances, litigation, inheritance, family, tenancy, mortgage, or possession risks?**

Karnataka’s Revenue Department / Bhoomi portal provides services including RTC and MR viewing, Khatha extract viewing, RTC information, Bhoomi mutation history, dispute-case reports, land-conversion services, survey-document viewing/requesting, revenue maps, RTC XML verification, and online mutation services.

**Source:** [Karnataka Revenue Department / Bhoomi services](https://landrecords.karnataka.gov.in/)

## C. RTC / Pahani / Mutation / 11E / Survey Check

For Karnataka, the portal should treat these as core land-record documents.

### RTC / Pahani

Shows landholder details, survey number, extent, classification, cultivation and crop-related details, and other revenue-record information.

### Mutation Register / MR

Shows revenue-record changes after sale, inheritance, partition, gift, court order, or other legal events.

### 11E Sketch

Important when part of an agricultural survey number is being sold, gifted, partitioned, or subdivided. It helps identify the exact portion and boundary before mutation.

### Tippan / Survey Sketch / Atlas / Revenue Map

Important for boundary, measurement, and survey verification. These help compare recorded extent, physical boundaries, and seller’s possession.

**Important:**  
RTC and revenue records are important but should not be treated as final proof of title by themselves. PRS notes that Indian land records can be spread across departments and may not always match ground reality; discrepancies in records are common where surveys and updates are incomplete.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

# 5. Land-Use Conversion & Development Rules

## Agricultural Land to Non-Agricultural Use

If the buyer wants to use land for farmhouse development, plotted development, resort, warehouse, commercial use, industrial use, renewable energy, school/college, residential layout, or any non-farming construction, the transaction should be routed through a **land-conversion and planning-approval check**.

Karnataka’s Revenue Department portal includes land-conversion services such as:

- Apply online for land conversion
- View conversion request status
- Download final conversion orders

**Source:** [Karnataka Revenue Department / land conversion services](https://landrecords.karnataka.gov.in/)

The portal should ask:

- Is the land inside an approved master-plan / planning area?
- Is it outside a master-plan area?
- Is the buyer intending agricultural use only?
- Is farmhouse/residential/commercial/industrial construction planned?
- Is the land part of a farm-plot project or revenue layout?
- Has land conversion been obtained?
- Has layout approval been obtained?
- Has the relevant planning authority / local body approval been obtained?
- Is the road width sufficient?
- Is there legal road access?
- Is the parcel a small subdivided agricultural parcel requiring 11E/phodi/durasth review?
- Are there lake-buffer, rajakaluve, forest, hill, or environmental restrictions?

## Farm Plots / Revenue Sites / Layout Risk

Farm plots and revenue sites around Bengaluru, Mysuru, Mangaluru, Hubballi-Dharwad, Belagavi, Tumakuru, Chikkaballapur, Devanahalli, Ramanagara, and other growth corridors require extra review.

The portal should check:

- Whether the land is still agricultural in RTC
- Whether conversion order exists
- Whether layout approval exists
- Whether individual plots are legally formed
- Whether 11E sketches were properly issued
- Whether roads/common areas are legally transferred
- Whether the land is being marketed as “farm land” but used as a residential layout
- Whether any RERA / planning authority / local body compliance applies

# 6. Special Risk Categories in Karnataka

## High-Risk Land Types

The portal should automatically flag these land types for lawyer review:

1. **PTCL / granted land**  
   Karnataka has special restrictions around certain lands granted to Scheduled Castes / Scheduled Tribes. The 2020 amendment inserted Section 80-A stating that no conditions laid down in the Land Reforms Act shall be relaxed for lands granted during the prohibition period under the Karnataka Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prohibition of Transfer of Certain Lands) Act, 1978.

   **Source:** [Karnataka Land Reforms (Amendment) Ordinance, 2020](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_KA_71_596_00003_10_1551858304230&filename=13_of_2020-ordinance.pdf&type=ordinance)

2. **Government / gomala / kharab / tank-bed / lake-buffer land**  
   Do not proceed unless land classification is clearly verified.

3. **Forest / deemed forest / eco-sensitive land**  
   Requires forest, environmental, and planning review.

4. **A-class irrigated land**  
   Review usage restrictions and whether the buyer’s intended use is agricultural or non-agricultural.

5. **Farm plot / revenue layout land**  
   Check conversion, planning approval, legal plot formation, road handover, and 11E/phodi/durasth documents.

6. **Inherited family land**  
   Check legal heirship, partition, release deeds, family settlement, and consent from all co-owners.

7. **Power of Attorney sale**  
   Verify POA validity, registration, scope, revocation, and whether the principal is alive and competent.

8. **Recently mutated land**  
   Check whether mutation follows a valid registered transfer and whether objections/disputes exist.

9. **Boundary mismatch land**  
   Compare RTC extent, sale deed extent, 11E sketch, survey sketch, revenue map, and physical possession.

10. **Land inside high-growth corridors**  
   Review conversion, acquisition notifications, road widening, planning authority restrictions, and development-zone impact.

# 8. Legal Risk Score Model for Karnataka

Use this for your portal’s automated risk scoring.

## Ownership Risk

**Low risk:** Clear title chain, seller name matches sale deed, RTC, and mutation records.  
**Medium risk:** Minor mismatch, missing parent document, recent mutation, or unclear co-owner consent.  
**High risk:** Seller name mismatch, inheritance dispute, POA issue, disputed possession, or missing title chain.

## Land Record Risk

**Low risk:** RTC/Pahani, mutation history, 11E/survey sketch, and sale deed match.  
**Medium risk:** Minor extent mismatch, subdivision pending, or survey document missing.  
**High risk:** Survey-number mismatch, hissa mismatch, 11E issue, government/gomala/kharab/forest flag, or boundary dispute.

## Encumbrance Risk

**Low risk:** Long EC is clean and title chain is consistent.  
**Medium risk:** Old mortgage released but supporting release documents are missing.  
**High risk:** Active mortgage, attachment, litigation entry, suspicious recent transfer, or unexplained title gap.

## PTCL / Granted-Land Risk

**Low risk:** No indication of grant/PTCL history after document review.  
**Medium risk:** Old grant reference or unclear grant conditions.  
**High risk:** SC/ST granted land, non-alienation condition, missing permission, or prior PTCL proceedings.

## Land Use Risk

**Low risk:** Buyer intends farming and land is private agricultural land.  
**Medium risk:** Farmhouse or minor construction planned.  
**High risk:** Plotting, resort, warehouse, commercial, industrial, or residential layout use without conversion/planning approval.

## NRI/FEMA Risk

**Low risk:** Indian resident buyer.  
**Medium risk:** NRI inheriting land and seeking transfer guidance.  
**High risk:** NRI/OCI attempting to purchase agricultural land, plantation, or farmhouse directly.

## FAQ: Can an NRI buy agricultural land in Karnataka?

Generally, no. NRIs/PIOs do not have general permission to purchase agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property in India. Such cases require specific legal review and may require approval.

**Source:** [MEA guidance on acquisition and transfer of immovable property in India](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf)

---

## FAQ: Is RTC enough to prove ownership in Karnataka?

No. RTC/Pahani is important, but it should be read with sale deeds, title chain, mutation records, EC, 11E/survey sketch, tax receipts, possession records, and land classification. In India, land ownership is generally presumptive, and government records are not always a guaranteed title.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

---

## FAQ: What documents should I check before buying agricultural land in Karnataka?

Check sale deed, parent documents, RTC/Pahani, mutation records, Encumbrance Certificate, 11E sketch where applicable, survey sketch/Tippan/Atlas, revenue map, tax receipts, access road proof, PTCL/granted-land status, and land-conversion order if non-agricultural use is intended.

---

## FAQ: What is 11E sketch in Karnataka land transactions?

An 11E sketch is important when a portion of an agricultural survey number is being sold, gifted, partitioned, or otherwise separately identified. It helps establish the exact portion and boundary for mutation and survey purposes. The portal should request it whenever the listing is not for the full survey-number extent.

---

## FAQ: What should I check before buying a farm plot in Karnataka?

Check whether the land is converted, whether the layout is approved, whether 11E/phodi/durasth records exist, whether roads and common areas are legally provided, whether khata/e-khata/Form 9/Form 11 documents are valid where applicable, and whether planning authority/local body approvals exist.
