# 1. Short State Summary Card

**Maharashtra is a restricted agricultural-land state. Raw agricultural land purchases by non-agriculturists usually require careful legal review, and in many cases Collector permission or a permitted exception. Every transaction should be checked for agriculturist status, Section 63 applicability, 7/12 extract, 8A, Ferfar/mutation entries, title chain, eSearch/Index II, land ceiling, land-use classification, Occupant Class-II/new-tenure status, tribal-land restrictions, access, survey boundaries, and conversion/planning approvals.**

Maharashtra land purchases should not be evaluated only from a generic “buyer wants land” angle. The larger risks are usually **whether the buyer is legally eligible, whether the seller has clear and transferable title, whether the 7/12 extract and mutation entries match the sale deed and possession, whether the land is affected by tenancy restrictions, whether it is Occupant Class-II / new-tenure / tribal / government / inam / trust / forest / ceiling-sensitive land, whether non-agricultural use is permitted, and whether farm plot or farmhouse development has valid planning approvals**.

Section 63 of the Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act bars sale, gift, exchange, lease, or possessory mortgage of agricultural land in favour of a person who is not an agriculturist, unless the Act or Collector permission allows it. The same section also contains important exceptions for land within municipal/planning authority areas or land allocated for non-agricultural use in a regional plan, development plan, or town-planning scheme, but such transfers carry use-condition and timeline risks.

## Result: Non-Agriculturist Indian Resident

**Preliminary result: Restricted for raw agricultural land. Legal review required before proceeding.**

Maharashtra is not like an open agricultural-land state for non-agriculturist buyers. Section 63 of the Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act bars transfer of agricultural land in favour of a non-agriculturist unless the Act permits it or the Collector / authorised officer grants permission subject to prescribed conditions.

A non-agriculturist buyer may still have possible routes in limited cases, such as:

- land covered by a valid non-agricultural use exception under Section 63(1C);
- land situated within specified municipal/planning authority limits;
- land allocated for residential, commercial, industrial, or other non-agricultural use in a draft/final regional plan, development plan, or town-planning scheme;
- bona fide industrial or integrated township project route under Section 63-IA;
- specific permission from the Collector or authorised officer.

However, these routes are conditional and require legal review. The portal should not show a simple “eligible” result for a non-agriculturist buying raw agricultural land in Maharashtra.

**Recommended next step:**  
Run Maharashtra Buyer Eligibility Review before paying token advance or signing an agreement.

## Result: NRI / OCI Buyer

**Preliminary result: Restricted for agricultural land, plantation property, and farmhouse purchase. Legal review required.**

NRIs and persons of Indian origin are generally permitted to acquire residential and commercial immovable property in India, but the general permission does **not** cover agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property. The Ministry of External Affairs’ guidance states that an NRI can purchase immovable property in India other than agricultural land, plantation property, or farm house, and that acquisition of agricultural land / plantation property / farmhouse by NRI/PIO requires specific approval.

An NRI/PIO may inherit agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property, but transfer of such inherited property is restricted; the MEA guidance says agricultural land / plantation property / farmhouse acquired by inheritance may be transferred only to Indian citizens permanently residing in India.

**Source:** [MEA guidance on acquisition and transfer of immovable property in India](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf)

**Recommended next step:**  
Do not proceed with purchase without a FEMA + Maharashtra land-law review.

## Result: Foreign National

The MEA guidance states that foreign nationals of non-Indian origin resident outside India cannot purchase immovable property in India and may only take residential accommodation on lease for a period not exceeding five years without RBI permission.

# 3. Maharashtra Legal Guidance: Main Content

## Buying Agricultural Land in Maharashtra: What You Must Check

Buying agricultural land in Maharashtra is different from buying an apartment or ordinary urban plot. A buyer should not rely only on a listing, agent statement, or the current 7/12 holder name.

In India, land ownership is generally presumptive: PRS notes that ownership is primarily established through registered sale deeds and supporting records, but these documents are not a government-guaranteed title, and the burden of checking past ownership usually falls on the buyer.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

For Maharashtra, the practical legal check should start with seven questions:

1. **Is the buyer legally eligible under Maharashtra agricultural land rules?**
2. **Does Section 63 restrict this transaction?**
3. **Is the seller legally entitled to sell, and does the title chain support that?**
4. **Do the 7/12 extract, 8A extract, Ferfar/mutation entries, sale deed, eSearch/Index II records, and possession match each other?**
5. **Is the land free from tenancy, ceiling, Occupant Class-II, tribal, inam, trust, government, gairan, forest, or other special restrictions?**
6. **Is the intended use agricultural, farmhouse, plotting, industrial, township, resort, warehouse, solar, or other non-agricultural use?**
7. **If non-agricultural use is intended, is it permitted under the regional/development plan and supported by valid approvals?**

MahaBhulekh provides online access to 7/12 extract, 8A extract and property card records for Maharashtra. The portal also allows users to select Record of Rights type, district, taluka, village, survey/gat number or name, and language.

**Source:** [MahaBhulekh: Maharashtra 7/12, 8A and Property Card services](https://bhulekh.mahabhumi.gov.in/)

Maharashtra IGR eSearch provides online paid search/download access to registration-related data/documents, but its own instructions state that extracted data is for information purpose only, is not claimed to be complete in every respect, and is not an ownership certificate. For certified copies or original information, users are directed to the concerned Sub Registrar Office.

**Source:** [Maharashtra IGR eSearch](https://esearchigr.maharashtra.gov.in/)

## C. 7/12 / 8A / Ferfar / Property Card Check

For Maharashtra, the portal should treat these as core land-record documents.

### 7/12 Extract

The 7/12 extract is the primary rural Record of Rights document. It generally shows survey/gat details, holder/cultivator details, land area, land type, cultivation/crop entries, and other revenue remarks. It is important, but not enough by itself to prove safe title.

### 8A Extract

8A is useful for account-wise landholding details and should be checked to understand the seller’s broader landholding, account number, and consistency with 7/12 records.

### Ferfar / Mutation Entries

Ferfar/mutation records show changes in land records after sale, inheritance, partition, gift, court order, or other events. A mutation entry should be supported by the underlying registered document, inheritance document, court order, or lawful basis.

### Property Card

For certain urban or city survey properties, the property card may be more relevant than a rural 7/12 extract.

**Important:**  
7/12 and revenue records are important but should not be treated as final proof of title by themselves. PRS notes that Indian land records can be spread across departments and may not always match ground reality; discrepancies in records are common where surveys and updates are incomplete.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

# 5. Land-Use Conversion & Development Rules

## Agricultural Land to Non-Agricultural Use

If the buyer wants to use land for farmhouse development, plotted development, resort, warehouse, commercial use, industrial use, township, solar project, or other non-farming construction, the transaction should be routed through a **land-use and planning approval check**.

Maharashtra has important statutory pathways and exceptions:

1. **Section 63 restriction for non-agriculturists:** Raw agricultural land transfer to a non-agriculturist is generally restricted unless permission or a statutory exception applies.
2. **Section 63(1C) planning/urban exception:** Section 63 does not apply to land situated within municipal corporation/municipal council limits, Special Planning Authority or New Town Development Authority jurisdiction, and also to land allocated to residential, commercial, industrial or other non-agricultural use in a draft/final regional plan or town-planning scheme.
3. **Use-condition risk:** Where land is transferred for non-agricultural use under the planning exception, the land must be put to the permitted non-agricultural use within the statutory timeline. Failure can trigger non-utilization charges, transfer restrictions, or resumption consequences.
4. **Section 63-IA industrial/township route:** Land may be sold without Collector permission to an agriculturist or non-agriculturist for bona fide industrial use or integrated township projects where the statutory conditions are met.

**Source:** [India Code PDF: Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, Section 63 and 63-IA](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19824/1/_tenancy_and_agricultural.pdf)

The portal should ask:

- Is the buyer an agriculturist?
- Is the land raw agricultural land?
- Is the land inside a municipal corporation or municipal council area?
- Is the land inside a Special Planning Authority or New Town Development Authority jurisdiction?
- Is the land allocated for residential, commercial, industrial, or other non-agricultural use in a draft/final regional plan, development plan, or town-planning scheme?
- Is the buyer claiming industrial use or integrated township use?
- Is farmhouse/residential/commercial construction planned?
- Is it part of a farm-plot or plotted development project?
- Has NA/conversion approval or planning permission been obtained where required?
- Has layout approval been obtained?
- Is road width/legal access sufficient?
- Are there eco-sensitive, forest, hill station, CRZ, tribal, or Class-II restrictions?

# 6. Special Risk Categories in Maharashtra

## High-Risk Land Types

The portal should automatically flag these land types for lawyer review:

1. **Raw agricultural land being bought by a non-agriculturist**  
   Section 63 risk. Do not allow a simple “eligible” output.

2. **Land relying on Section 63(1C) exception**  
   Check municipal/planning authority status, plan allocation, permitted non-agricultural use, use timeline, and entries in Record of Rights.

3. **Industrial / integrated township route under Section 63-IA**  
   Check statutory conditions, land location, permitted use, utilization timeline, charges, Occupant Class-II implications, eco-sensitive restrictions, and Collector compliance.

4. **Occupant Class-II / new-tenure land**  
   Check transferability, permission, premium/nazarana, conversion order, and tenure conversion status.

5. **Tribal / Scheduled Tribe land**  
   Section 63-IA itself notes that land owned by a Scheduled Tribe person is subject to Maharashtra Land Revenue Code sections 36 and 36A and the Maharashtra Restoration of Lands to Scheduled Tribes Act, 1974. Treat all tribal-land transfers as high-risk.

6. **Tenanted / tenant-purchase-sensitive land**  
   Check tillers’ day, tenant purchase, protected tenant, and restrictions under tenancy law.

7. **Section 43 restricted land**  
   Land purchased or sold under the Tenancy Act may have transfer restrictions. Check previous tenant-purchase orders and permissions.

8. **Ceiling-sensitive land**  
   Check whether the transaction creates exposure under the Maharashtra Agricultural Lands (Ceiling on Holdings) Act, 1961.

9. **Government / gairan / nazul / public land**  
   Do not proceed unless classification and authority to sell are conclusively verified.

10. **Inam / watan / service / trust / temple / wakf land**  
    Check title, authority to sell, transfer restrictions, and permissions.

11. **Forest / eco-sensitive / hill station / CRZ-sensitive land**  
    Verify forest classification, eco-sensitive zone restrictions, hill station/tourism rules, coastal restrictions, and planning permissions.

12. **Farm plot projects / revenue layouts / gunthewari-type plots**  
    Check land-use, layout approval, access road, subdivision legality, planning authority approval, and whether marketing triggers RERA/project compliance.

13. **Inherited family land**  
    Check legal heirship, succession, partition, release deeds, family settlement, and consent of all co-owners.

14. **Power of Attorney sale**  
    Verify POA validity, scope, registration, revocation status, and whether the principal is alive and competent.

15. **7/12 / 8A / Ferfar mismatch land**  
    Compare 7/12, 8A, mutation entries, sale deed, eSearch/Index II, survey/gat number, area, and physical possession.

# 13. SEO Content Blocks

## FAQ: Can a non-farmer buy agricultural land in Maharashtra?

A non-farmer / non-agriculturist should not assume they can freely buy raw agricultural land in Maharashtra. Section 63 of the Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act generally bars transfer of agricultural land in favour of a non-agriculturist unless permission or a valid statutory exception applies. Some urban/planning/non-agricultural-use exceptions may exist, but they require careful legal review.

**Source:** [India Code PDF: Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19824/1/_tenancy_and_agricultural.pdf)

---

## FAQ: Can an NRI buy agricultural land in Maharashtra?

Generally, no. NRIs/PIOs do not have general permission to purchase agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property in India. Such cases require specific legal review and may require approval.

**Source:** [MEA guidance on acquisition and transfer of immovable property in India](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf)

---

## FAQ: Is 7/12 enough to prove ownership in Maharashtra?

No. 7/12 is important, but it should be read with the sale deed, parent title documents, 8A, Ferfar/mutation entries, eSearch/Index II records, survey/gat records, tax records, possession evidence, and any permissions or restrictions. In India, land ownership is generally presumptive, and government records are not always a guaranteed title.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

---

## FAQ: What is 8A in Maharashtra land records?

8A is an important account-wise landholding record that should be checked along with 7/12. It helps verify landholding details and consistency between the seller’s account and the parcel being sold.

**Source:** [MahaBhulekh: Maharashtra 7/12, 8A and Property Card services](https://bhulekh.mahabhumi.gov.in/)

---

## FAQ: What is Ferfar / mutation in Maharashtra?

Ferfar or mutation entries record changes in revenue records after events such as sale, inheritance, partition, release, gift, or court order. Mutation should not be accepted blindly; it must be backed by the underlying legal document or order.

---

## FAQ: What should I check before buying a farm plot in Maharashtra?

Check buyer eligibility, Section 63 applicability, land-use status, 7/12, 8A, Ferfar, eSearch/Index II, title chain, survey/gat details, road access, NA/conversion approval, layout approval, planning authority permissions, Occupant Class-II or tribal restrictions, and whether the project triggers RERA or other project compliance.

---

## FAQ: Can agricultural land in Maharashtra be bought for industrial use?

There may be a route under Section 63-IA for bona fide industrial use or integrated township projects, subject to statutory conditions. The land location, planning permissibility, utilization timeline, charges, Occupant Class-II implications, eco-sensitive restrictions, and Collector compliance should be checked before proceeding.

**Source:** [India Code PDF: Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, Section 63-IA](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19824/1/_tenancy_and_agricultural.pdf)
