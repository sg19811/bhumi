# 1. Short State Summary Card

**Kerala is legally sensitive for agricultural and rural land because transactions often require checks under land ceiling rules, paddy/wetland restrictions, land classification, conversion permission, title chain, survey records, mutation/Pokkuvaravu, and environmental or coastal/hill-area restrictions.**

For Indian resident buyers, Kerala does not usually operate like a simple “farmer-only purchase” state. However, Kerala has strong land ceiling and land-use control frameworks. The most important checks are usually:

- Whether the seller has valid and saleable title.
- Whether the land is private freehold land or restricted land.
- Whether the land is recorded as paddy land, wetland, nilam, purayidam, garden land, plantation, or other classification.
- Whether the land is included in the Paddy/Wetland Data Bank.
- Whether land conversion or change of nature is required.
- Whether the land is affected by Kerala Land Reforms ceiling restrictions.
- Whether the land falls under coastal, hill, forest, plantation, ecologically sensitive, or tribal-area restrictions.
- Whether the Basic Tax Register, Thandaper, possession certificate, FMB/survey records, and sale deed match.
- Whether the Encumbrance Certificate and previous title chain are clean.

The Kerala Survey and Land Records Department states that old survey records and ground realities may differ because many changes from land transactions were not reflected in records, and a fresh survey became necessary to resolve boundary and rights disputes. This makes survey/FMB and ground-verification particularly important in Kerala. ([dslr.kerala.gov.in](https://dslr.kerala.gov.in/))

The Kerala Registration Department also explains that registration laws help prove document authenticity, publicize transactions, prevent fraud, and help verify whether property has previously been transferred. ([registration.kerala.gov.in](https://registration.kerala.gov.in/))

**Portal classification:**  
**Indian resident buyer:** Usually proceed to due diligence.  
**NRI/OCI buyer:** High restriction for agricultural land / plantation / farmhouse purchase.  
**Company/LLP/trust buyer:** Legal review required because of ceiling, land-use, entity purpose, and FEMA/FDI issues.  
**Paddy/wetland/Data Bank land:** High-risk; lawyer and RDO/conversion review required.  
**Farmhouse/farm plot/project land:** Check land-use, Data Bank status, conversion, building/planning permissions, road access, and local-body approvals.  
**Plantation land:** Check ceiling exemptions, land-use restrictions, title, access, and environmental/forest/hill-area issues.

# 3. Kerala Legal Guidance: Main Content

## Buying Agricultural Land in Kerala: What You Must Check

Buying agricultural land, plantation land, or rural property in Kerala requires a different due-diligence approach from buying an apartment or urban residential plot. A buyer should not rely only on a broker statement, tax receipt, possession certificate, or revenue classification.

In India, land ownership is generally presumptive. PRS explains that ownership is primarily established through registered sale deeds and supporting records, but these documents are not a state-guaranteed title, and the burden of checking past ownership usually falls on the buyer. ([prsindia.org](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india))

For Kerala, the practical legal check should begin with six questions:

1. **Is the seller legally entitled to sell?**
2. **Does the title chain match the revenue records and possession?**
3. **Is the land classified as purayidam, nilam, paddy land, wetland, plantation, garden land, or another category?**
4. **Is the land included in the Paddy/Wetland Data Bank or affected by the Kerala Conservation of Paddy Land and Wetland Act, 2008?**
5. **Does the purchase violate Kerala Land Reforms ceiling restrictions or require an exemption?**
6. **Can the land be used for the buyer’s intended purpose — farming, farmhouse, farm plot, plantation, eco-tourism, resort, warehouse, or investment?**

Kerala High Court-related reporting also highlights a recurring practical issue: revenue records alone may not always reflect the full physical nature or use of land, and authorities may need to consider title documents, physical characteristics, actual use, locality, access, and fair value information in relevant land-classification/compensation contexts. ([timesofindia.indiatimes.com](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/kochi/dont-rely-on-revenue-records-alone-while-fixing-compensation-for-acquired-land-rules-kerala-high-court/articleshow/120905396.cms))

## C. Thandaper / BTR / Pokkuvaravu / FMB Check

For Kerala, the portal should treat these as core land-record checks.

**Thandaper:**  
Revenue account/holder reference used in Kerala land tax and revenue records.

**Basic Tax Register (BTR):**  
Important revenue record showing classification, survey number, extent, tax, and land details.

**Pokkuvaravu / Mutation:**  
Revenue mutation process after transfer, inheritance, partition, or other changes.

**Possession Certificate:**  
Revenue certificate commonly used to show possession details.

**Survey/FMB/Resurvey Records:**  
Boundary and measurement records. Kerala’s Survey and Land Records Department notes that old survey records and current land realities may differ because changes from land transactions were not always reflected in survey records, and resurvey is meant to address boundary and rights disputes. ([dslr.kerala.gov.in](https://dslr.kerala.gov.in/))

**Important:**  
Revenue records are important but should not be treated as final proof of title by themselves. They must be read together with registered deeds, title chain, possession, survey records, EC, and physical inspection.

# 5. Land-Use Conversion & Development Rules

## Agricultural / Paddy / Wetland to Non-Agricultural Use

If the buyer wants to use land for farmhouse development, plotted development, resort, warehouse, commercial use, industrial use, tourism, construction, or non-farming use, the transaction should be routed through a **land-use, Data Bank, and conversion approval check**.

Kerala has a strong paddy/wetland protection framework under the Kerala Conservation of Paddy Land and Wetland Act, 2008. Recent High Court-related reporting shows that Revenue Divisional Officers have an important role in land reclassification/change-of-nature applications, and that in some cases involving unnotified land above 20.2 ares, 10% may need to be set apart for water conservancy measures under Section 27A-related workflow. ([timesofindia.indiatimes.com](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/kochi/only-rdo-can-demarcate-area-for-water-conservancy-rules-kerala-hc/articleshow/122980978.cms))

The portal should ask:

- Is the land recorded as nilam/paddy/wetland?
- Is it included in the Data Bank?
- Has the land been removed from the Data Bank, if required?
- Is a KSRSEC/KSREC or satellite imagery report relevant?
- Is the land already physically converted?
- Was conversion before or after the paddy/wetland law?
- Has the RDO passed an order?
- Is the land extent above threshold limits for special conditions?
- Is 10% water conservancy set-apart applicable?
- Is the intended use farmhouse, plotting, resort, commercial, industrial, or construction?
- Does the local body/building permit allow the intended construction?
- Is CRZ, forest, hill-area, or environmental approval relevant?

For paddy/wetland-sensitive land, do not show “safe to buy” without lawyer review and document verification.

# 6. Special Risk Categories in Kerala

## High-Risk Land Types

The portal should automatically flag these land types for lawyer review:

1. **Nilam / paddy land / wetland / Data Bank land**  
   Check Data Bank status, physical nature, conversion history, RDO orders, and paddy/wetland law implications.

2. **Land classification mismatch**  
   If the deed, BTR, possession certificate, tax receipt, and ground reality differ, require legal and survey review.

3. **Converted land without clear order**  
   If land appears converted but no valid order is available, flag as high risk.

4. **Plantation / estate land**  
   Check title, ceiling exemption, plantation status, environmental restrictions, access, and fragmentation risk.

5. **Land ceiling-sensitive holdings**  
   Kerala Land Reforms ceiling rules may affect large landholding, entity purchase, plantations, and aggregation.

6. **Assigned land / patta land with conditions**  
   Check transfer restrictions and government assignment terms.

7. **Forest / hill / eco-sensitive / Western Ghats-sensitive land**  
   Verify forest notifications, environmental restrictions, and building/development restrictions.

8. **Coastal Regulation Zone-sensitive land**  
   Check CRZ restrictions before buying coastal or backwater property.

9. **Tribal or protected-category land**  
   Check transfer restrictions, local permissions, and special laws.

10. **Devaswom / temple / church / trust land**  
   Check authority to sell, title, permission, and institutional restrictions.

11. **Inherited family land**  
   Check legal heirship, partition, release deeds, and consent of all co-owners.

12. **Power of Attorney sale**  
   Verify POA validity, registration, scope, revocation, and whether principal is alive and competent.

13. **Survey/boundary mismatch land**  
   Compare survey/FMB/resurvey records, BTR extent, deed extent, possession, and physical boundaries.

# 8. Legal Risk Score Model for Kerala

Use this for your portal’s automated risk scoring.

## Ownership Risk

**Low risk:** Clear title chain, seller name matches deeds, tax, possession, mutation, and revenue records.  
**Medium risk:** Minor mismatch, old deed gaps, recent mutation, missing parent deed.  
**High risk:** Seller name mismatch, unclear inheritance, POA issue, co-owner dispute, disputed possession.

## Land Record Risk

**Low risk:** BTR, Thandaper, possession certificate, survey/FMB, and sale deed match.  
**Medium risk:** Minor extent mismatch or resurvey pending.  
**High risk:** Survey mismatch, classification mismatch, missing FMB, ground-revenue record mismatch.

## Paddy/Wetland/Data Bank Risk

**Low risk:** Not paddy/wetland/Data Bank land; classification clean.  
**Medium risk:** Classification ambiguity or historical conversion claim.  
**High risk:** Nilam/Data Bank/wetland entry, conversion without clear order, pending RDO proceedings.

## Encumbrance Risk

**Low risk:** Long EC clean and title chain consistent.  
**Medium risk:** Old mortgage released but release documents missing.  
**High risk:** Active mortgage, attachment, litigation entry, suspicious recent transfer, registered dispute.

## Land Use Risk

**Low risk:** Buyer intends existing lawful agricultural/residential use with no conversion.  
**Medium risk:** Farmhouse or small construction planned, approvals pending.  
**High risk:** Plotting, resort, commercial, warehouse, industrial, or non-agri use without land-use/conversion/local-body approvals.

## Ceiling / Entity Risk

**Low risk:** Small individual purchase with no obvious ceiling issue.  
**Medium risk:** Multiple holdings, family aggregation, or large purchase.  
**High risk:** Company/LLP/trust/large estate/plantation transaction without ceiling and exemption review.

## NRI/FEMA Risk

**Low risk:** Indian resident buyer.  
**Medium risk:** NRI inheriting land and seeking transfer guidance.  
**High risk:** NRI/OCI attempting to purchase agricultural land, plantation, or farmhouse directly.
