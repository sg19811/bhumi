# 1. Short State Summary Card

**Tamil Nadu is generally treated as a more open state for Indian resident buyers of private agricultural land, but every transaction must be checked for title, Patta/Chitta, Encumbrance Certificate, land-use classification, ceiling limits, access, survey boundaries, and special-category restrictions.**

Tamil Nadu land purchases should not be evaluated only from a “buyer eligibility” angle. The bigger risks are usually **whether the seller has valid title, whether the land record matches the ground reality, whether the land is private land or government/poramboke land, whether conversion or planning permission is required, and whether the land has inheritance, encumbrance, layout, road-access, or survey issues**.

Tamil Nadu’s DTCP advisory asks buyers to check the seller’s right over the property, verify Patta and Encumbrance Certificate, verify approved layout status where relevant, check road/park handover, verify land-use zone, and obtain planning/building approvals where applicable.

**Source:** [Tamil Nadu DTCP buyer advisory](https://tcp.tn.gov.in/before%20buying%20of%20individual%20plot%20%20residential%20building)

For an Indian resident individual, Tamil Nadu does not usually function like a strict “farmer-only purchase” state for ordinary private agricultural land. However, eligibility is only the first layer. Before proceeding, verify the seller’s title, Patta/Chitta, A-Register, FMB sketch, Encumbrance Certificate, land classification, access road, survey boundaries, co-owner consent, and whether any conversion or planning permission is needed.

---

## Result: NRI / OCI Buyer

**Preliminary result: Restricted for agricultural land, plantation property, and farmhouse purchase. Legal review required.**

NRIs and persons of Indian origin are generally permitted to acquire residential and commercial immovable property in India, but the general permission does **not** cover agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property. The Ministry of External Affairs’ guidance states that an NRI can purchase immovable property in India other than agricultural land, plantation property, or farm house, and that acquisition of agricultural land / plantation property / farmhouse by NRI/PIO requires specific approval.

An NRI/PIO may inherit agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property, but transfer of such inherited property is restricted; the MEA guidance says agricultural land / plantation property / farmhouse acquired by inheritance may be transferred only to Indian citizens permanently residing in India.

**Source:** [MEA guidance on acquisition and transfer of immovable property in India](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf)

**Recommended next step:**  
Do not proceed with purchase without a FEMA + Tamil Nadu land-law review.

## Result: Foreign National

**Preliminary result: Not eligible without specific legal approval.**

Foreign nationals of non-Indian origin resident outside India are generally not permitted to purchase immovable property in India, except limited cases such as inheritance, and specific RBI approval may be required. The MEA guidance states that foreign nationals of non-Indian origin resident outside India cannot purchase immovable property in India and may only take residential accommodation on lease for a period not exceeding five years without RBI permission.

**Source:** [MEA guidance on acquisition and transfer of immovable property in India](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf)

# 3. Tamil Nadu Legal Guidance: Main Content

## Buying Agricultural Land in Tamil Nadu: What You Must Check

Buying agricultural land in Tamil Nadu is different from buying an apartment or urban plot. A buyer should not rely only on the listing, Patta name, or seller statement.

In India, land ownership is generally presumptive: PRS notes that ownership is primarily established through registered sale deeds and supporting records, but these documents are not a government-guaranteed title, and the burden of checking past ownership usually falls on the buyer.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

For Tamil Nadu, the practical legal check should start with five questions:

1. **Is the seller legally entitled to sell?**
2. **Does the Patta/Chitta and A-Register match the sale deed and ground possession?**
3. **Is the land private patta land, or is it government / poramboke / assigned / restricted land?**
4. **Is the buyer allowed to acquire this land type?**
5. **Can the land be used for the buyer’s intended purpose — farming, farmhouse, farm plot, development, resort, warehouse, or investment?**

Tamil Nadu’s land-record e-services portal allows users to view Patta/Chitta/FMB, A-Register extracts, verify Patta/Chitta, verify government/private/poramboke land status, download FMB sketches, view Patta order copies, and apply for Patta transfer. The portal also states that the information is dynamically updated and that a physical visit to a Revenue Department office is not required to obtain information generated through the e-service.

**Source:** [Tamil Nadu e-Services of Land Records](https://eservices.tn.gov.in/eservicesnew/index.html)

## B. Seller Title Check

Before accepting a listing as “verified,” ask for:

- Parent document / mother deed
- Latest sale deed
- Previous title chain
- Patta / Chitta
- A-Register extract
- FMB sketch
- Encumbrance Certificate
- Tax receipts
- Mutation / Patta transfer records
- Legal heirship or partition documents, if inherited land
- Power of Attorney, if seller is represented by another person
- Co-owner consent, if joint ownership exists

The DTCP advisory for buyers specifically recommends checking whether the seller has the right over the property, verifying Patta and Encumbrance Certificate, checking layout approval where applicable, verifying land-use zone, and ensuring planning permissions are obtained for construction.

**Source:** [Tamil Nadu DTCP buyer advisory](https://tcp.tn.gov.in/before%20buying%20of%20individual%20plot%20%20residential%20building)

---

## C. Patta / Chitta / A-Register / FMB Check

For Tamil Nadu, the portal should treat these as core land-record documents.

### Patta / Chitta

Shows revenue-record details and current recorded holder.

### A-Register

Shows classification, extent, survey number, and revenue details.

### FMB Sketch

Field Measurement Book sketch showing survey boundaries and measurements.

**Important:**  
Patta and revenue records are important but should not be treated as final proof of title by themselves. PRS notes that Indian land records can be spread across departments and may not always match ground reality; discrepancies in records are common where surveys and updates are incomplete.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)

---

## D. Encumbrance Certificate Check

The Encumbrance Certificate should be checked for:

- Prior sale transactions
- Mortgages
- Settlement deeds
- Gift deeds
- Release deeds
- Court attachments
- Loans or charges
- Suspicious recent transfers
- Gaps in title chain

If the EC period is too short, recommend a longer EC search, typically covering at least 30 years where practical.

# 5. Land-Use Conversion & Development Rules

## Agricultural Land to Non-Agricultural Use

If the buyer wants to use land for farmhouse development, plotted development, resort, warehouse, commercial use, industrial use, or non-farming construction, the transaction should be routed through a **land-use and planning approval check**.

Tamil Nadu DTCP lists **G.O. Ms. No. 79 — Rules for change of land use from Agriculture to Non-agriculture in Non-Planned Areas** and the **Tamil Nadu Combined Development Regulations and Building Rules, 2019** among state rules relevant to development and land-use permissions.

**Source:** [Tamil Nadu DTCP State Rules](https://tcp.tn.gov.in/staterules)

The portal should ask:

- Is the land inside a planning area?
- Is it in a non-planning area?
- Is the buyer intending agricultural use only?
- Is farmhouse/residential/commercial construction planned?
- Is it part of a layout or farm-plot project?
- Has DTCP/local body approval been obtained?
- Is the road width sufficient?
- Has the land-use zone been verified?

DTCP’s buyer advisory also tells buyers to verify the land-use zone as per the master plan and obtain planning permission from DTCP and building approval from the concerned local body where construction is involved.

**Source:** [Tamil Nadu DTCP buyer advisory](https://tcp.tn.gov.in/before%20buying%20of%20individual%20plot%20%20residential%20building)

# 6. Special Risk Categories in Tamil Nadu

## High-Risk Land Types

The portal should automatically flag these land types for lawyer review:

1. **Poramboke / government-classified land**  
   Do not proceed unless classification is clarified through official records.

2. **Assigned land / conditional grant land**  
   Check whether transfer is prohibited or restricted.

3. **Temple / trust / wakf-related land**  
   Check title, authority to sell, and any restrictions.

4. **Inam / ryotwari conversion land**  
   Check whether patta is valid and saleable.

5. **Forest / hill area / eco-sensitive land**  
   Verify forest classification, hill area restrictions, and planning approvals.

6. **Farm plot projects**  
   Check layout approval, road handover, land-use conversion, and development permissions.

7. **Inherited family land**  
   Check legal heirship, partition, release deeds, and consent of all co-owners.

8. **Power of Attorney sale**  
   Verify POA validity, scope, registration, and whether principal is alive and competent.

9. **Recently mutated land**  
   Check whether mutation follows a valid registered transfer.

10. **Boundary mismatch land**  
    Compare FMB sketch, survey number, Patta extent, sale deed extent, and physical possession.

## FAQ: Can an NRI buy agricultural land in Tamil Nadu?

Generally, no. NRIs/PIOs do not have general permission to purchase agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property in India. Such cases require specific legal review and may require approval.

No. Patta is important, but it should be read with sale deeds, title chain, A-Register, FMB sketch, EC, tax receipts, and possession records. In India, land ownership is generally presumptive, and government records are not always a guaranteed title.

**Source:** [PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india)
