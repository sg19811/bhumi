# 1. Short State Summary Card

**Andhra Pradesh is generally treated as a relatively open state for Indian resident buyers of private agricultural land, but every transaction must be checked for land ceiling exposure, seller title, Adangal, ROR-1B, LP Map/FMB, mutation, encumbrance, assigned/DKT land status, dotted land status, Scheduled Area restrictions, road access, survey boundaries, and conversion/planning approvals.**

Andhra Pradesh land purchases should not be evaluated only from a simple “buyer eligibility” angle. The larger risks are usually **whether the seller has clear and transferable title, whether MeeBhoomi revenue records match the registered title documents and ground possession, whether the land is assigned/DKT/government/poramboke/dotted/ceiling-surplus/tribal/Scheduled Area-sensitive land, whether conversion is required for the intended use, and whether farmhouse, farm plot, layout, resort, industrial, solar, warehouse, or institutional use has valid planning and land-use approvals**.

The official MeeBhoomi portal of the Andhra Pradesh Revenue Department provides public land-record services and refers to Adangal, ROR-1B, LP Maps, Pattadar and related land-record services. The portal also lists revenue services such as Mutation for Transactions, Mutation for Correction, Aadhaar seeding, and Land Conversion for Non Agriculture Purpose. ([MeeBhoomi, Government of Andhra Pradesh](https://meebhoomi.ap.gov.in/))

Because Andhra Pradesh land-conversion rules have been undergoing policy changes, the portal should show a **current-rule verification warning** for non-agricultural use. Reports in 2025 stated that the state government was preparing to repeal/simplify the Andhra Pradesh Agricultural Land (Conversion for Non-Agricultural Purposes) Act, 2006 / NALA framework and move toward a more seamless conversion process. Therefore, for every non-agricultural-use case, the latest official Government Order, local-body process, development permission, and current conversion fee/tax position must be verified before advice is final. # 3. Andhra Pradesh Legal Guidance: Main Content

## Buying Agricultural Land in Andhra Pradesh: What You Must Check

Buying agricultural land in Andhra Pradesh is different from buying an apartment or urban plot. A buyer should not rely only on the listing, survey number, Adangal, ROR-1B, or seller statement. In India, land ownership is generally presumptive: PRS notes that ownership is primarily established through registered sale deeds and supporting records, but these documents are not a government-guaranteed title, and the burden of checking past ownership usually falls on the buyer. ([PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india))

For Andhra Pradesh, the practical legal check should start with six questions:

1. **Is the seller legally entitled to sell?**
2. **Do the sale deed, Adangal, ROR-1B, mutation records, LP Map/FMB and possession match?**
3. **Is the land private patta land, or is it assigned/DKT/government/poramboke/dotted/ceiling-surplus/restricted land?**
4. **Is the buyer allowed to acquire the land under Indian law, FEMA, ceiling rules and any special state restriction?**
5. **Can the land be used for the buyer’s intended purpose: farming, farmhouse, farm plot, resort, solar, warehouse, development, industrial, or investment?**
6. **Is the land in a Scheduled Area, tribal-sensitive belt, coastal zone, forest-sensitive area, irrigation-command area, or urban-development/planning authority area?**

The official MeeBhoomi portal describes itself as the public portal for Andhra Pradesh land records and displays revenue services such as Mutation for Transactions, Mutation for Correction, Aadhaar seeding, and Land Conversion For Non Agriculture Purpose. It also references Adangal, ROR-1B, LP Maps, Pattadar and related services. ([MeeBhoomi](https://meebhoomi.ap.gov.in/))

## C. Adangal / ROR-1B / LP Map / FMB / Mutation Check

For Andhra Pradesh, the portal should treat these as core land-record documents.

### Adangal

Adangal is a village/revenue record commonly used to show survey details, land extent, classification, cultivation/possession details and other land-use information. It is important for land verification, but it should be checked against the registered title documents and ground position.

### ROR-1B / 1-B Extract

ROR-1B is a Record of Rights style record that shows pattadar/khata details, land extent, survey/subdivision references and related revenue details. It is a crucial document for preliminary verification, but it should not be treated as the only proof of ownership.

### LP Map / FMB / Survey Sketch

LP Map, FMB or survey sketch helps identify the parcel geometry, boundary references, survey subdivision and physical location. For the portal, this should feed into parcel-boundary verification and area mismatch detection.

### Mutation Records

Mutation updates revenue records after transfer, inheritance, partition or correction. Mutation is important for revenue-record alignment, but it does not replace title verification through registered deeds and link documents.

**Important:** PRS notes that Indian land records can be spread across departments and may not always match the ground position; discrepancies in records are common where surveys and updates are incomplete. ([PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india))

# 5. Land-Use Conversion & Development Rules

## Agricultural Land to Non-Agricultural Use

If the buyer wants to use land for farmhouse development, plotted development, resort, warehouse, commercial use, industrial use, solar park, institutional use, or non-farming construction, the transaction should be routed through a **land-use and planning approval check**.

The portal should ask:

- Is the land being purchased only for agricultural use?
- Is the buyer planning farmhouse or residential construction?
- Is the buyer planning a plotted/farm-plot layout?
- Is the land inside a municipality, urban development authority, CRDA, or planning authority area?
- Is the land in a village panchayat / non-planning area?
- Is agricultural-to-non-agricultural conversion required under the current Andhra Pradesh framework?
- Has the latest NALA/current land-use conversion process been verified?
- Has building/layout/development permission been obtained where required?
- Is the road width sufficient?
- Is the land affected by irrigation, tank-bed, canal, water body, coastal, forest, hill, tribal, assigned/DKT, or dotted-land restrictions?

Because Andhra Pradesh’s land-conversion framework has been under active change, the system should not show a static final answer for non-agricultural use. Instead, it should show:

**“Land-use conversion rules in Andhra Pradesh have been changing. Please verify the latest Government Order / local-body process / development permission requirement before proceeding. Lawyer review recommended.”**

Reports in 2025 stated that the state government had initiated steps to repeal or simplify the NALA Act and allow a more seamless conversion process linked to tax/payment and development-permission workflows. ([Times of India, June 2025](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/vijayawada/andhra-pradesh-moves-to-repeal-nALA-act-plans-seamless-land-conversion/articleshow/121785298.cms))

## Farm Plots / Rural Layout / Farmhouse Risk

Farm plot and farmhouse transactions in Andhra Pradesh require extra caution because sellers may market agricultural land as investment plots without valid conversion, layout approval, road access, common-area handover, drainage access, or building permission.

For every farm plot / farmhouse / rural layout listing, require:

- Title chain
- Adangal and ROR-1B
- LP Map / FMB / survey sketch
- EC
- Conversion approval or latest conversion-process confirmation
- Layout approval / local body / planning authority approval where applicable
- Road access proof
- Drainage / water access proof where applicable
- Common-area / road handover documents where applicable
- RERA/project review if marketed as a plotted development project
- Seller/developer authorization and entity documents

# 6. Special Risk Categories in Andhra Pradesh

## High-Risk Land Types

The portal should automatically flag these land types for lawyer review:

1. **Assigned / DKT land**  
   Many assigned lands carry transfer restrictions or resumption risk. Do not treat assigned/DKT land as freely transferable without checking assignment conditions, freehold/regularisation status, and current revenue records.

2. **Government / poramboke land**  
   Do not proceed unless classification is clarified through official records and a lawyer confirms transferability.

3. **Dotted land**  
   Dotted land may involve uncertainty in revenue records and ownership entries. Require revenue-record and title-chain review.

4. **Scheduled Area / tribal land**  
   Critical legal risk. Transactions in Scheduled Areas or tribal-sensitive belts require specialist review before any listing is promoted or lead is routed.

5. **Inam / endowment / temple / trust / wakf-sensitive land**  
   Check title, authority to sell, alienation restrictions, and endowment/revenue approvals.

6. **Ceiling-surplus / land-reform-sensitive land**  
   Review ceiling history, land reform proceedings, exemptions, and surplus declarations if any.

7. **Forest / hill / eco-sensitive / coastal land**  
   Verify classification, environmental restrictions, coastal regulation zone, hill area, forest proximity, and local planning rules.

8. **Tank-bed / water-body / canal / irrigation-sensitive land**  
   Check whether any part of the parcel is water-body, tank-bed, canal margin, drainage course, or irrigation department land.

9. **Farm plot / rural layout projects**  
   Check conversion, layout approval, road access, planning permission, marketing claims, and RERA/plotted development implications where applicable.

10. **Inherited family land**  
   Check legal heirship, succession, partition, release deeds, and consent from all co-owners.

11. **Power of Attorney sale**  
   Verify POA validity, scope, registration, revocation status, and whether principal is alive and competent.

12. **Recently mutated land**  
   Check whether mutation follows a valid registered transfer and whether any appeal/correction/litigation is pending.

13. **Boundary mismatch land**  
   Compare sale deed extent, Adangal extent, ROR-1B extent, LP Map/FMB, survey subdivision, and physical possession.

# 8. Legal Risk Score Model for Andhra Pradesh

Use this for your portal’s automated risk scoring.

## Ownership Risk

**Low risk:** Clear title chain, seller name matches registered deed, Adangal/ROR-1B and possession.  
**Medium risk:** Minor mismatch, missing link document, recent mutation, inherited land without full documentation.  
**High risk:** Seller name mismatch, unclear inheritance, POA issue, disputed possession, missing title chain.

## Land Record Risk

**Low risk:** Adangal, ROR-1B, LP Map/FMB, sale deed, survey number and possession broadly match.  
**Medium risk:** Minor extent mismatch, pending mutation, subdivision issue.  
**High risk:** Survey number mismatch, assigned/DKT/government/poramboke/dotted flag, unclear boundaries.

## Encumbrance Risk

**Low risk:** Long EC clean and title chain consistent.  
**Medium risk:** Old mortgage released but release document missing, short EC period.  
**High risk:** Active mortgage, attachment, litigation entry, suspicious recent transfer, multiple POA transfers.

## Assigned / DKT / Government-Land Risk

**Low risk:** Land clearly private transferable patta land.  
**Medium risk:** Old assignment history but freehold/regularisation documents appear available.  
**High risk:** Assigned/DKT/government/poramboke land, transfer conditions unclear, or freehold status disputed.

## Dotted / Revenue Correction Risk

**Low risk:** No dotted-land or revenue-record uncertainty.  
**Medium risk:** Historical discrepancy corrected by official order.  
**High risk:** Dotted entry unresolved, ownership unclear in resettlement/revenue records, or mutation pending.

## Land Use Risk

**Low risk:** Buyer intends farming and land is recorded as private agricultural land.  
**Medium risk:** Farmhouse or small construction planned but conversion/planning route appears feasible.  
**High risk:** Plotting, resort, warehouse, commercial, industrial, institutional, or solar use without conversion/planning approval.

## Scheduled Area / Tribal Risk

**Low risk:** Land is outside Scheduled Area and not tribal-restriction sensitive.  
**Medium risk:** Near Scheduled Area or ownership history requires clarification.  
**High risk:** Scheduled Area / tribal land / 1 of 70 regulation risk / non-tribal transfer concern.

## NRI/FEMA Risk

**Low risk:** Indian resident buyer.  
**Medium risk:** NRI inheriting land and seeking transfer guidance.  
**High risk:** NRI/OCI attempting to purchase agricultural land, plantation, or farmhouse directly.

# 13. SEO Content Blocks

## FAQ: Can a non-farmer buy agricultural land in Andhra Pradesh?

Andhra Pradesh is generally treated as more open than states that impose strict agriculturist-only purchase rules. However, the buyer should verify land ceiling exposure, title, Adangal, ROR-1B, LP Map/FMB, mutation, EC, land classification, assigned/DKT status, dotted land status, Scheduled Area restrictions, and intended land use before proceeding.

---

## FAQ: Can an NRI buy agricultural land in Andhra Pradesh?

Generally, no. NRIs/PIOs do not have general permission to purchase agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse property in India. Such cases require specific legal review and may require approval. ([MEA guidance](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf))

---

## FAQ: Is Adangal enough to prove ownership in Andhra Pradesh?

No. Adangal is important, but it should be read with sale deeds, title chain, ROR-1B, LP Map/FMB, mutation records, EC, tax receipts, and possession records. In India, land ownership is generally presumptive, and government records are not always a guaranteed title. ([PRS: Land Records and Titles in India](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india))

---

## FAQ: What is ROR-1B in Andhra Pradesh land records?

ROR-1B is a key revenue record showing pattadar/khata and land details. It helps verify revenue-record status but must be cross-checked with registered sale deeds, link documents, mutation records, LP Map/FMB, EC and possession.

---

## FAQ: What should I check before buying a farm plot in Andhra Pradesh?

Check seller title, Adangal, ROR-1B, LP Map/FMB, mutation records, EC, conversion status, layout approval, local body/planning authority approval, road access, common-area handover, developer authority, and whether RERA/project registration review is needed.

---

## FAQ: What is assigned or DKT land in Andhra Pradesh?

Assigned/DKT land generally refers to land granted by the government, often to landless or weaker-section beneficiaries, subject to conditions. Such land may carry transfer restrictions, resumption risk, or special approval requirements. The portal should always route assigned/DKT land to lawyer and revenue-record review.

---

## FAQ: What is dotted land risk in Andhra Pradesh?

Dotted land refers to land where historical revenue records may show uncertainty or missing ownership particulars. Such land requires detailed revenue-record review, correction/regularisation records, title-chain reconciliation, and lawyer opinion before any transaction.
